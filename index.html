The Westside Court Boyz (WCB, Courtside 162, or Viet Courtside) is an emerging East Asian street gang and criminal enterprise operating primarily out of public housing projects and apartment complexes centered around the Imagination Courts bordering Vespucci and Del Perro in West Los Santos.

As a smaller and upcoming set striving to make a name for itself, WCB sits on the lower rungs of the underworld hierarchy. While striving to connect with larger, established criminal players in the city, the gang currently operates mostly on the periphery, attempting to build capital and reputation through street level resourcefulness.

Origins & Sociological Background

The roots of the Westside Court Boyz trace back to the arrival of second wave Southeast Asian refugees during the late 1970s and early 1980s. Unlike first wave migrants who fled immediately following the Fall of Saigon in 1975, who were predominantly urban, educated, and possessed familiarity with Western administrative systems, second wave arrivals consisted largely of rural working class families and boat people who survived hazardous ocean crossings and extended stays in refugee camps across Hong Kong and the Philippines. Over 50 percent of these second wave arrivals were young children or teenagers, many of whom migrated without intact family units due to limited family financial resources.

Upon resettlement in the low income public housing complexes of West Los Santos, young Vietnamese refugees confronted severe poverty, cultural isolation, and linguistic barriers. Parents often worked grueling hours in nail salons, meatpacking facilities, or low wage manufacturing to escape federal public assistance. Left without structured community support and subjected to extortion and physical bullying by established neighborhood gangs, teenager groups centered around the Imagination Courts housing blocks formed mutual defense cliques. Over time, these street defense networks absorbed older criminal influences and formalized into the Westside Court Boyz.

Schooling & Street Socialization

Field research and school district records indicate that local public middle and high schools functioned as primary sites of interethnic conflict for WCB members. Within West Los Santos secondary schools, Vietnamese refugee youths were routinely targeted due to physical stature, limited English proficiency, refugee status, and distinct dress styles. Tensions frequently escalated into campus wide race fights between Asian and Latino student populations, prompting police lockdowns, expulsions, and hate crime charges.

Alienated by institutional neglect, high dropout rates, and disciplinary exclusions, many members received their primary socialization on the streets rather than in classrooms. High rates of institutional movement, shuttling between public high schools, continuation programs, juvenile detention halls, and state probation programs, further alienated youths from formal education, cementing peer group solidarity and street reliance over academic engagement.

Territory & Pride

The Westside Court Boyz take intense pride in their home turf, maintaining their primary cultural base, origin site, and everyday lives directly in and around the Imagination Courts near Vespucci Beach. Members live, hang out, and raise their families within these exact housing blocks, anchoring the set's deep-rooted sense of community loyalty.

While their core identity is fiercely tied to this specific neighborhood, WCB operates as a mobile network when it comes to business. Driven by financial rackets rather than rigid block defense, members move freely across municipal borders throughout West Los Santos. Their criminal operations and social networks extend across the Del Perro commercial strip, Rockford Hills luxury corridors, South Central transport yards, and regional satellite cliques along interstate highway corridors reaching northern San Andreas and out of state hubs.

Etymology & The "162" Identifier

The numeric tag "162" holds historical significance within the set's identity, representing Block 162, the specific public housing structure within the Imagination Courts complex where the gang's original founders first organized. In street graffiti, institutional tagging, and tattoo flash, "162" is frequently paired with "WCB" or "C 162" (Courtside 162) to signify allegiance to the original Vespucci housing cluster, even as members operate across non spatial, citywide mobile networks.

Style, Tattooing & Law Enforcement Profiling

During the 1990s and 2000s, WCB members broke away from traditional suit and tie gang aesthetics, adopting West Coast street apparel characterized by crimson red accents, Dickies clothing, oversized work jackets, Nike Cortez footwear, and shaved heads.

A signature element of WCB attire is the frequent wearing of red Washington Nationals baseball caps. The cursive "W" logo is adopted on clothing and embedded into tattoo work to represent "West" (West Side / West Courtside), serving as a subtle street signifier.

State & Federal Prison Politics: The Other Car (TOC)

In state and federal correctional facilities across San Andreas, inmate populations are deeply stratified by rigid racial and geographic coalitions. Within these concrete walls, The Other Car (commonly abbreviated as TOC) refers to a collective group or coalition made up of inmates who do not fit into the dominant demographic factions (such as White, Black, or Hispanic groups).

  • Composition: It typically includes Asian Americans, Pacific Islanders, Native Americans, foreign nationals, and non-affiliated individuals.
  • Population Share: As one of the smallest groups on the yard, it generally accounts for roughly 6% or less of the general inmate population depending on the specific facility.
  • Function: Unlike structured major criminal cartels or dominant prison gangs, TOC functions primarily for mutual survival, everyday unit security, and basic self-regulation rather than managing an expansive criminal empire.

When Westside Court Boyz members end up doing time in state or federal facilities, they hook into The Other Car (TOC) on a super low level. Because WCB is a small, upcoming neighborhood set trying to find its footing on the streets, its members carry little weight inside prison yard politics. They align with TOC strictly for fundamental protection and yard survival, keeping a low profile while attempting to learn the ropes from older, more seasoned inmates without getting entangled in heavy yard wars.

Criminal Rackets & Network Ambitions

Law enforcement intelligence identifies WCB as an enterprise prioritizing non territorial financial gain, though they remain small fish trying to catch the eye of larger city syndicates:

  • Vehicle Theft & Export: Hijacking and hacking ignition systems of luxury vehicles in nearby Del Perro and Richman, which are subsequently processed through low-level chop shops. WCB aims to supply these vehicles upward to larger export rings operating around Los Santos Ports.
  • Weapons Conversion: Converting legally acquired semi automatic handguns into fully automatic firearms using imported drop in conversion switches to sell to mid-level brokers.
  • Synthetic Drug Supply: Operating low-level regional distribution networks for heavy methamphetamine and synthetic narcotics, trying to scale up their supply lines by courting higher-level connects.
