The West Courtside Boyz (WCB, Courtside 162, or Viet Courtside) is an East Asian street gang and criminal enterprise operating primarily out of public housing projects and apartment complexes centered around the Imagination Courts bordering Vespucci and Del Perro in West Los Santos.

The gang's demographic composition is overwhelmingly Vietnamese-American, with a minority representation of youths from Cambodian, Laotian, and other East/Southeast Asian backgrounds. Originally formed as a neighborhood self-defense network against inter-ethnic harassment in local public schools, WCB evolved into a mobile criminal network involved in vehicle theft rings, automatic firearms conversions, cyber fraud, and regional narcotics supply lines.

Origins & Sociological Background

The roots of the West Courtside Boyz trace back to the arrival of "second-wave" Southeast Asian refugees during the late 1970s and early 1980s. Unlike "first-wave" migrants who fled immediately following the Fall of Saigon in 1975—who were predominantly urban, educated, and possessed familiarity with Western administrative systems—second-wave arrivals consisted largely of rural working-class families and "boat people" who survived hazardous ocean crossings and extended stays in refugee camps across Hong Kong and the Philippines. Over 50 percent of these second-wave arrivals were young children or teenagers, many of whom migrated without intact family units due to limited family financial resources.

Upon resettlement in the low-income public housing complexes of West Los Santos, young Vietnamese refugees confronted severe poverty, cultural isolation, and linguistic barriers. Parents often worked grueling hours in nail salons, meatpacking facilities, or low-wage manufacturing to escape federal public assistance. Left without structured community support and subjected to extortion and physical bullying by established neighborhood gangs, teenager groups centered around the Imagination Courts housing blocks formed mutual defense cliques. Over time, these street defense networks absorbed older criminal influences and formalized into the West Courtside Boyz.

Schooling & Street Socialization

Field research and school district records indicate that local public middle and high schools functioned as primary sites of inter-ethnic conflict for WCB members. Within West Los Santos secondary schools, Vietnamese refugee youths were routinely targeted due to physical stature, limited English proficiency, refugee status, and distinct dress styles. Tensions frequently escalated into campus-wide "race fights" between Asian and Latino student populations, prompting police lock-downs, expulsions, and hate crime charges.

Alienated by institutional neglect, high dropout rates, and disciplinary exclusions, many members received their primary socialization on the streets rather than in classrooms. High rates of institutional movement—shuttling between public high schools, continuation programs, juvenile detention halls, and state probation programs—further alienated youths from formal education, cementing peer-group solidarity and street reliance over academic engagement.

Territory & Non-Spatial Mobility

Unlike traditional street gangs whose identities are strictly demarcated by specific street blocks or residential boundaries, the West Courtside Boyz do not adhere strictly to fixed geographic turf boundaries. While maintaining their primary cultural base and origin site at the Imagination Courts near Vespucci Beach, WCB operates as a highly fluid and mobile criminal network.

Driven by financial rackets rather than block defense, WCB affiliates move freely across municipal borders. Their criminal operations and social networks extend across the Del Perro commercial strip, Rockford Hills luxury corridors, South Central transport yards, and regional satellite cliques along interstate highway corridors reaching northern California and out-of-state hubs.

Style, Tattooing & Law Enforcement Profiling

During the 1990s and 2000s, WCB members broke away from traditional suit-and-tie gang aesthetics, adopting West Coast street apparel such as Dickies clothing, oversized work jackets, Nike Cortez footwear, and shaved heads.

  • Law Enforcement Injunctions: Due to their adoption of specific street apparel, gang members were frequently targeted by specialized Asian Gang Task Forces, Cal-Gang database logging, and gang injunction enhancements under anti-gang legislation.

Criminal Rackets

Law enforcement intelligence identifies WCB as an enterprise prioritizing non-territorial financial gain:

  • Vehicle Theft & Export: Hijacking and hacking ignition systems of luxury vehicles in nearby Del Perro and Richman, which are subsequently processed through illegal chop shops and exported via shipping containers at Los Santos Ports.
  • Weapons Conversion: Converting legally acquired semi-automatic handguns into fully automatic firearms using imported drop-in conversion switches.
  • Synthetic Drug Supply: Operating regional wholesale distribution networks for heavy methamphetamine and synthetic narcotics.
